# Sarce — Trajectory and maths review (October 2026)

**Status:** review of `staging` at `068b20c` (22 Oct 2026, after the prophylaxis lens). Findings are
proposals. The ones that change a domain invariant say which ADR they need.

Related: [BENCHMARK.md](./BENCHMARK.md) · [COMPUTE.md](./COMPUTE.md) · [LEGAL.md](./LEGAL.md) ·
[SUSTAINABILITY.md](./SUSTAINABILITY.md)

## 1. Where Sarce is heading

| | Then (June spec) | Now (October) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core idea | Tal-style Brilliant % tracker | A style fingerprint: Brilliant % headline plus nine traits (Sacrifice, Chaos, Calm, Initiative, Defence, Grip, Fight, Clock, Foresight) |
| Scope | Phase 1 MVP | Phases 1–3 done; chaos plan M0–M8 merged; web and Expo v1.0 screens QA'd |
| Engine | Stockfish (WASM + server) | Server only, plus Maia-2 for human move probabilities |
| Users | Personal | Multi-user (sign-in, shared links, opponent prep, Lichess import) |
| Validation | — | Chaos validated (M4: 50,516 bullet positions, held-out Brier 0.155). Nothing else validated |

**The direction is right.** "Who you are as a player" is a real gap: Chess.com and Lichess grade
accuracy, ChessBase profiles opponents, Maia predicts human moves, and nobody combines them into a personal
style mirror.

**The main risk is breadth outrunning validation.** Four new lenses (squeeze, swindle, clock, prophylaxis) and their traits shipped on 22 October alone.
Only one, chaos, has evidence that it measures what it says. Each unvalidated trait shown as a 0–100 number
is a claim about a person. If it's wrong, it's the fastest way to lose the "that's me" feeling the product
depends on. **Recommendation:** freeze new traits until §2's fixes and a per-lens validation (F9) are done.

## 2. Statistical and mathematical findings

Ranked by impact. Each lists where it is in the code and a proposed fix.

### F1 — Chess.com ratings go into a Lichess-trained model unconverted (high)

- **Where:**
  - `services/human_model.py` (`ELO_LADDER`, `elo_ladder`);
  - `services/brilliant_find_rate.py` (`rung_for`);
  - `lenses/chaos.py` (`nearest_elo`).
- **What:** Maia-2 was trained on Lichess games, so its "1500" is a Lichess 1500. Opponent ratings from
  Chess.com are used as-is. Chess.com ratings generally sit below Lichess ratings for the same strength, by an
  amount that varies with time control and level. A Chess.com 1500 is therefore modelled as a weaker Lichess
  player than they are:
  - **P_fail is overstated** for Chess.com games, so chaos is inflated;
  - the **find-rate clause** (ADR 0004) is judged against the wrong level.

  M4 validated on Lichess data only, so the calibration doesn't carry over automatically.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a `rating_to_model_elo(source, time_control, rating)` mapping, versioned and documented, used
    everywhere a rating meets the human model.
  - Start from published Chess.com↔Lichess comparisons. Refine it with players who have accounts on both
    sites (opt-in in the pilot).
  - Bump the lens and style-vector versions when it lands.

### F2 — The Sacrifice trait still uses the loose sacrifice test (high)

- **Where:**
  - `services/move_signals.py`: the `"sacrifice"` signal is `material_sacrifice(board, move) > 0`;
  - `services/you.py` (`trait_values` → "Sacrifice").
- **What:** ADR 0007 tightened Brilliant to `real_offer`, because `material_sacrifice` counts any piece a reply
  *could* land on, defended or not. It even counts pawns, since the test is `> 0`. The Sacrifice axis still uses
  the loose test. The symptom is in `data/reference_axes.json`: **Morphy 22, Shirov 20, Tal 18.** The
  greatest sacrificer scores lowest, which suggests the axis is measuring piece activity rather than
  sacrifice.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a `real_sacrifice` signal: `real_offer(board, move) >= 3`, and later `>= 1` for pawn gambits as their
    own bucket.
  - Base the trait on it, bump the style-vector version, rescore the references, and check that Tal ranks
    where he should.

### F3 — Reference players aren't comparable to the player (high)

- **Where:** `data/reference_axes.json`, `scripts/process_tal_chaos.py`.
- **What:**
  1. All three references were scored with `"model": "heuristic"`, but players' chaos uses Maia-2. M4 showed the
     heuristic is badly calibrated (Brier 0.414 unadjusted). So the Tal dot and your dot are on different scales.
  2. Only 40 games each. A trait at 20% over 40 games has a 95% interval of roughly 10–35%. That's wider than the
     gaps the You screen draws.
  3. They were classical games against GMs, scored with a blitz-trained model whose ladder tops out at 2400,
     and with no clock adjustment.
  4. Three very different attackers come out almost identical (chaos 36/31/36, calm 96/90/95). The axes may not
     separate styles at all.
- **Fix:**
  - Rescore with Maia-2 on at least 200 games per player, and store the interval next to each value.
  - Add **contrasting references**: Petrosian, Karpov and Carlsen. If Petrosian scores like Tal on Chaos, the
    axis is measuring something other than style (a discriminant validity check).
  - Label the dots "Tal (classical)", so nobody reads them as a bullet target.

### F4 — Small samples, no uncertainty shown (high)

- **Where:** `services/home.py` (`MIN_GAMES = 5`), the You trend, and `baseline.get_timeline` (per-game
  Brilliant %).
- **What:** Brilliant is rare, so its percentage is very noisy. A bullet game has about 35 user moves, and five
  games about 175. Binomial ranges for the *observed* rate, given the true rate:

  | Games (≈ moves) | True 2% → 95% of observed values | True 6% → 95% of observed values |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 5 (175) | 0.0 – 4.6% | 2.9 – 9.7% |
  | 20 (700) | 1.0 – 3.1% | — |
  | 50 (1,750) | 1.4 – 2.7% | — |

  After five games, a 2% player and a 6% player can show the same number. Per-game Brilliant % on the
  timeline is mostly 0 with spikes. "You improved" claims made from that are mostly noise.
- **Fix:**
  - Show Brilliant % with an interval: Wilson, or a Beta posterior shrunk toward the player's rating-band prior
    (beta-binomial).
  - Draw the trend as a rolling window of about 20 games, not one point per game.
  - Only say "up" or "down" when the windows' intervals separate.
  - Apply the same rule to every trait. Show *n* (for example, "Grip · 14 winning positions") and dash any
    trait with fewer than about 30 events, not 5 games.

### F5 — The Brilliant rule differs from its spec in three places (medium)

- **Where:** `services/brilliant.py`, `services/move_classifier.py`.
  1. **"Not forced"** is `len(legal_moves) > 1`, which almost never excludes anything. The spec says *other
     reasonable options existed*. Decide which is meant. If the sacrifice is the only move that holds, many
     players would call that the most brilliant case. Write the choice into an ADR and test it.
  2. **Lost positions:** only `eval_before > winning_margin` is excluded. A sacrifice at −6 that "holds" −6
     counts as Brilliant. Use the same decided band as chaos (`DECIDED_LOW/HIGH` in EP) on both sides.
  3. **Classification uses raw centipawn loss** (`THRESHOLDS`). Losing 100 cp at +6 and at 0.0 get the same
     label, but only the second matters. Lichess judges by a win-chance drop, and Chess.com uses an
     expected-points model. Labelling by **EP loss**, which `ep.py` already computes, would agree better with
     both, and would put Phase 1 and the lenses on one scale. This changes the meaning of the tiers, so it
     needs an ADR and a style-vector bump.

### F6 — The 6–10% target has never been checked against the current rules (medium)

- **What:** 6–10% means about 2–4 Brilliants in *every* 35-move bullet game. It was set before ADR 0004
  (find rate) and ADR 0007 (real offer) made Brilliant much stricter, and Tal's own Brilliant % hasn't been
  re-measured under rule v3. The band is a CI-enforced contract (`test_philosophy_contracts.py`).
- **Fix:**
  - Re-run `process_tal_benchmark.py` under v3 rules on at least 200 Tal games, and publish Tal's rate with an
    interval.
  - Keep 6–10% as the aspirational "Tal-tier" band if Tal is near it. If he isn't, change the band in an ADR.
  - Either way, add a personal **next band**: the player's current interval plus one step. That's a goal they
    can reach this month.

### F7 — Winner's curse in "best" lists (medium)

- **Where:** `services/chaos_map.py` (the sound move "that asks the most"), and "most chaotic first".
- **What:** picking the maximum of many noisy estimates systematically picks overestimates. An opening position
  seen three times will often top the list by luck.
- **Fix:**
  - Rank by a shrunk estimate: the lower confidence bound, or a Bayesian mean toward the band average.
  - Require a minimum number of sightings.
  - Show "seen n times". The trap atlas has the same issue (most traps rest on 3–4 sightings, VALIDATION.md)
    and should use the same treatment.

### F8 — Mixed units across lenses (medium)

- **Where:** `lenses/config.py`.
- **What:** chaos uses EP (CHAOS_PLAN rule 4). Squeeze, swindle, clock and prophylaxis use centipawns. Centipawns
  stretch near decided positions: 200 cp means different things at 0 and at +5. Squeeze (≥ +2) and swindle
  (≤ −2) live exactly in that region.
- **Fix:** express those thresholds in EP, as the chaos plan's own rule says, or document per lens why
  centipawns are intended.

### F9 — Only chaos is validated (medium)

- **What:**
  - M4 covered bullet (1+0) only, at 100k nodes against 1.5M in the app;
  - `CHAOS_THRESHOLD` and `SAFE_TOL` are still starting values;
  - squeeze, swindle, clock, prophylaxis, survival and the intention tagger have no validation.
- **Fix:** give every lens an M4-style test on the Lichess database, with a falsifiable prediction:

  | Lens | Prediction to test |
  | --- | --- |
  | Squeeze / Grip | "Gave air" moves lower the mover's conversion rate compared with "kept" moves in matched positions |
  | Swindle / Fight | "Set" positions raise the opponent's error rate above the baseline for that eval |
  | Prophylaxis / Foresight | "Let through" moves raise the rate at which the opponent's threat is executed |
  | Clock | "Rushed" in critical positions predicts a higher error rate than "found" at the same clock |
  | Chaos | Re-run for 3+0 and 10+0, and at the app's node budget |

### F10 — The traits overlap and have no reference point (medium)

- **Where:** `services/you.py` (`trait_values`), `services/reference_axes.py`.
- **What:**
  - Initiative is the mean chaos score, and Chaos is the share of chaos verdicts, so they're built from the same
    data and will be strongly correlated.
  - Calm sits at 90–96 for everyone (a ceiling effect).
  - The axes are raw fractions, so "36" has no meaning until you know what's normal at your rating.
- **Fix:**
  - Compute the correlation matrix across players and merge traits that move together (|r| > 0.8).
  - Turn each trait into a **percentile within the player's rating band and time control**, using the Lichess
    database. Then "Chaos 80" means "more than 80% of 1500 bullet players", which is the honest form of a
    fingerprint.
  - Store the population tables like the trap atlas: positions and counts only, no names.

### F11 — Smaller items

- The find-rate rule uses the **opponent's** rating as a stand-in for the mover's, and rounds to 400-point
  rungs (`rung_for`). Use the mover's own rating, converted per F1, at the rounded-to-100 rung that
  `elo_ladder` already adds for the lenses.
- The style label in `services/style_profile.py` uses hand-picked cut-offs (0.25, 0.3, ×2). Replace them with the
  percentile traits from F10, or delete it.
- The README phase table still says Phases 2 and 3 are planned (fixed in this PR).

## 3. Product and engineering

1. **Deploy.** Nothing is hosted yet. Deployment unlocks the pilot, the benchmark's Part D and any income.
   Suggested setup:
   - the API on a dedicated server with Stockfish;
   - managed Supabase in a region chosen with the PDPC transfer rules in mind ([LEGAL.md](./LEGAL.md));
   - the web app on a static/edge host.
2. **Time to first read.** A new player should see a real read within about two minutes of signing up.
   Analyse the last 20 games first, then backfill ([COMPUTE.md](./COMPUTE.md)).
3. **One thing to work on.** Nine traits is a lot. The Home screen should pick *one* focus each week (the
   style focus picker, ADR 0003, is the hook) and say how this week's games moved it.
4. **Operations:**
   - error tracking;
   - analysis queue metrics (depth, age, failures);
   - nightly database backups;
   - a status line in Settings when analysis is behind.
5. **Dependencies:** about a dozen open Dependabot PRs (Next 16, ESLint 10, TypeScript). The remaining "high"
   advisories need the breaking ESLint upgrade. Schedule one housekeeping PR a week, as M0 did.
6. **Branches:** `feat/background-import-jobs`, `feat/chaos-sorted-games` (superseded by #139) and
   `fix/service-role-grants` are unmerged and stale. Close them or merge them.

## 4. Brilliant disagreements with Chess.com

Players will see Chess.com call a move Brilliant when Sarce doesn't, and the reverse. Plan:

1. **Name it as Sarce's.** Show one line under the badge the first few times: "Sarce Brilliant — a real offer
   that held and was hard to find." Keep the classical !! glyph in Sarce colours, never Chess.com's look
   ([LEGAL.md](./LEGAL.md) §3).
2. **"Almost Brilliant."** When a move passes every clause but one, say which one, in coaching language:
   - "Beautiful idea, but the bishop was defended, so it was never really on offer."
   - "Sound and bold, but most 1500s find this, so it's Best, not Brilliant."

   "Why this move?" already has the checklist; this just surfaces the near-misses.
3. **"Chess.com called this Brilliant" button** on any move. Sarce shows which clause failed and stores the flag
   (move, clause). This becomes free, continuous calibration data for benchmark Part B.
4. **A public definition page** with three examples each of Brilliant, almost-Brilliant and not-Brilliant.
   Add the benchmark agreement numbers once Part B has run.

## 5. Staying the layer on top

Players play on Chess.com and Lichess. Sarce is where they understand themselves. To reinforce that:

- **Tagline:** "Play where you play. Understand yourself here."
- **Session reads:** after an auto-sync, notify the player ("Your last session's read is ready"). Bullet players
  think in sessions; Recap is weekly.
- **Link back:** every move in Review links to the same move on Chess.com or Lichess.
- **Export to a Lichess study:** the Lichess API can create studies, so Sarce annotations (intent notes,
  Brilliant reasons, chaos moments) can be exported straight into the player's Lichess.
- **Keep Play to training only:** bots, drills, "defend it yourself". Never rated games.
- **Respect fair play:** post-game only ([LEGAL.md](./LEGAL.md) §3).

## 6. Feature ideas

In rough order of value against effort:

1. **Style cards ("Chess Wrapped").** A shareable image or short animation of a player's fingerprint, best
   Brilliant and favourite chaos move, with the mascot. This is the main growth engine. Shared links already
   strip third-party names.
2. **Weekly focus quests.** "Play like Tal this week": one trait, a target based on the player's interval (F4),
   and three drills from their own games. Ends with a recap: did it move?
3. **Opponent-aware chaos.** Opponent prep plus the chaos map: "Against this player, your 5…d5 line is where
   they go wrong most." The pieces exist (`scouting.py`, `chaos_map.py`).
4. **Style twin.** The historical player closest to your fingerprint, shown *with uncertainty* (needs F3 and
   F10). For example: "Closest: Shirov (but your Grip is more Karpov)."
5. **Coach view.** Students' fingerprints side by side, assigning drills, and session notes. This is also the
   Coach tier ([SUSTAINABILITY.md](./SUSTAINABILITY.md)).
6. **Per-time-control fingerprints.** Bullet-you and rapid-you are different players. Show both, and the gap
   between them.
7. **Chaos budget (bullet).** How much objective risk you took per game against how much it paid, as a simple
   P&L: "You spent 0.4 EP on chaos and got 0.7 back."
8. **Localisation.** Kiswahili first: East African players are an underserved audience and the copy is short.
   Then Spanish, Hindi and Russian, where chess audiences are large.
9. **Voice or animated recap.** A 30-second weekly recap narrated by the mascot. It fits the brand's motion
   work (`lib/motion/`).
